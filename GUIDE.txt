WILDFIND
Swipe through abandoned places in the UK. Works with no internet.


OPENING IT
----------
Double click WildFind.html. It opens in your browser. That is the whole
install. Nothing runs in the background, nothing phones home, and it works
in aeroplane mode.

Everything it needs is in this folder, so you can copy the folder to a USB
stick or another machine and it will still work.


USING IT
--------
Near / distance      Type a town in the Near box and pick a distance. You can
                     also paste coordinates, like 53.38,-1.47
                     Or press the target button beside it to choose on a map:
                     drag the map until the middle is on the spot, set the
                     circle with the slider, then Use this area.
Kinds                The row of chips filters by type. Tap one to switch it
                     off. Scroll the row sideways for the rest.
Drag sideways        Right saves it to your list. Left hides it for good.
                     Only sites with real photographs are dealt.
                     The two buttons underneath do the same thing.
< and > on the photo Step through that site's photographs. Swiping up or down
                     on the picture does the same, and so do the up and down
                     arrow keys. None of it counts as a decision.
Saved                Your shortlist. Tick a site off once you have been, add
                     notes, or remove it. Sites are grouped by nearest town so
                     you can see which ones make a day out together.
List or Map          In Saved, Map shows your saved sites pinned on an outline
                     of Britain. Drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom, click a
                     pin for its details. Orange still to visit, hollow done.
Menu (top right)     Counts, deck order, dark mode, backups, and a way to
                     bring back everything you hid.

Keyboard: left and right arrows decide, up and down step through photographs.


GETTING THE REAL DATA IN
------------------------
The GitHub copy comes with the full UK site list but no photographs, which
are too big to keep there. Until you fetch them, each card shows a labelled
sketch of that kind of structure.

First time only: install Python. Open the Microsoft Store, search for
Python 3.12, click Get. That is all; the script handles the rest.

Then double click:

    tools\FETCH DATA.bat

Two stages, and you can stop either with Ctrl+C and resume later:

    Stage 1  osm      the whole UK site list     a few minutes, plus the download
    Stage 2  photos   photographs                about half a day

Stage 1 downloads a 2.1 GB extract of OpenStreetMap, filters it on your
machine, and then deletes the extract again. What is left is a few megabytes
of data files. After that you have every location offline with grid
references, which is the useful part. Stage 2 is the long one and is slow on
purpose (see below). It is capped at 35,000 sites and 9.5 GB, and is worth
leaving running over a night or two.

Only want one stage:

    tools\FETCH DATA.bat -Stage osm
    tools\FETCH DATA.bat -Stage photos

Want more or fewer photographs:

    tools\FETCH DATA.bat -Stage photos -MaxPhotoGB 6 -PhotosPerSite 2

Keep the 2.1 GB extract instead of deleting it:

    tools\FETCH DATA.bat -Stage osm -KeepExtract


WHY A BULK EXTRACT AND NOT A LIVE API
-------------------------------------
The obvious way to get this data is the Overpass API, which answers live
queries against OpenStreetMap. The first version of this script did exactly
that, and it does not work at this scale. Overpass is a free service shared
by everyone, and its own error page says the public endpoint is for
browser-based and human-operated tools, and that heavy or automated users
should run their own instance. Asking it for fifty thousand features meant
single queries running for over ten minutes and mostly returning "at
capacity".

Geofabrik publishes a complete extract of the UK every night for precisely
this purpose. Downloading it once and filtering locally is faster, complete
rather than partial, and does not lean on a service that is not meant for
it. That is why there is a Python step: reading the extract needs the osmium
library, which the script installs for you.


HOW THE PHOTO STAGE STAYS POLITE
--------------------------------
Photographs could be found by asking Commons "what is near here?" 35,000
times. Wikimedia's robot policy says not to: bulk data should come from
their dumps, not the live API. Geograph, where most of the photos come from,
says the same thing more bluntly about its own site.

So the photo stage:

  - Downloads Commons' list of where every photograph was taken, once
    (about 770 MB), and does all the matching on your machine. That replaces
    35,000 searches with one file.
  - Keeps each site's shortlist (a few MB) and deletes the big list before
    any images download, so the run stays inside 10 GB.
  - Asks the live API only for what the list cannot give - each file's
    address, author and licence - fifty files per request, and tells the
    API to refuse it whenever Wikimedia's databases are under strain.
  - Downloads one image at a time, never in parallel, over one connection
    kept open between requests. After each one it waits 0.2 s plus half as
    long as that answer took, so it slows down whenever the servers do,
    and it backs right off if asked to.
  - Asks for 640 px copies and shrinks them to 500 px on your machine.
    Most 640 px copies already exist on Wikimedia's servers (many Geograph
    originals are 640 px to begin with), whereas 500 px ones mostly have to
    be made on request, which measured at 0.6-1.2 s of their servers' time
    per photo. Downloading a little more data spares them that work.
  - Never touches Geograph's own servers. Its photos reach Commons through
    Geograph's bulk upload, which is the route they ask people to use.

If your internet drops part way, or anything else stops the photo stage,
it waits five minutes and tries again, for up to 12 hours, so a run left
overnight carries on by itself. Close the window to stop it for good.


THE NUMBERS
-----------
From OpenStreetMap's own tag statistics for the UK:

    historic=ruins      26,814 features   only 3,739 of them named
    building=ruins      13,900
    military=bunker      3,460
    man_made=mineshaft   3,273
    plus mines, forts, adits, abandoned railways and more

So the pool is comfortably over 50,000. Only about 14% carry a name, so this
app includes the unnamed ones too. An unnamed mineshaft is still a real place
you can walk to; it just gets described rather than named, as

    Mineshaft near Wirksworth

with its grid reference underneath. Named sites are sorted to the front of
the photo queue, because they match their photographs far better.

Photographs are kept at 500 px wide, three per site, and are capped at
35,000 sites and 9.5 GB. Expect the photos folder to end up around
6-9 GB. The run downloads roughly three times that over the internet,
because each 640 px copy is shrunk after it arrives.

Disk is not really the limit. Time is: about a day of downloading, which
it does happily across several sittings.


WHERE THE DATA COMES FROM
-------------------------
Sites and towns    OpenStreetMap, from Geofabrik's nightly UK and Isle of
                   Man extracts. Open Database Licence. Real, community
                   surveyed positions.
Photographs        Wikimedia Commons, mostly Geograph photographs under
                   CC BY-SA. Every picture's author and licence is shown on
                   the card and listed in ATTRIBUTION.txt.

Not 28DayLater or Urbexology. Their write ups and photographs are other
people's copyright and their terms do not allow bulk copying, so this project
does not scrape them. 28DL also deliberately withholds
coordinates, so even a full copy would give you stories without locations,
which is the one thing this app actually needs.


HOW GOOD ARE THE PHOTOGRAPHS
----------------------------
Photographs are found by position, then kept only if their title names the
site. A random sample of the first night's results (every nearby photo,
named or not) showed why: about half were roads, fields and views that
happened to be close by. So now:

  - A photo is kept only when at least half of the distinctive words in
    the site's name appear in its title. A word that is just the name of
    a town or village within 10 km is ignored, so "Quarter Collieries"
    does not pick up photos of Quarter village.
  - Unnamed sites get no photographs: there is nothing to match.
  - Photos downloaded before this rule that do not name their site were
    moved to the photos-removed folder. Nothing was deleted; once you are
    happy, delete that folder.

After the first full run, 8,124 sites have at least one real photograph
(a third of the named ones). Only those are dealt into the swipe deck. The
other sites stay in the data, so a later, wider photo search can add more,
and anything you saved earlier stays in Saved. If the data has no
photographs at all, every site is dealt and shows a labelled sketch of that
kind of structure instead.

Title matching is not perfect. A photo can name the site and still show the
road outside it, and a good photo with a vague title is missed. But it is
by far the most reliable signal there is.


YOUR SAVES
----------
Saves, ticks and notes are stored by your browser, for this folder, on this
machine. They are not in any file you can see, so:

  - Clearing browsing data can wipe them.
  - They do not follow the folder to another machine on their own.

Menu > Save a backup file writes them to a .json you can keep or move, and
Load a backup file reads one back.


BEFORE YOU GO ANYWHERE
----------------------
A position on a map is not permission to be there and not a statement that a
place is safe. Most of these are on private land. Mines and quarries in
particular kill people: bad air, flooded levels, rotten floors, old
explosives. Derelict buildings bring asbestos and collapse. Check access and
tell someone where you are going.


IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------
Nothing shows up
  data\locations.js is missing or empty. Run tools\FETCH DATA.bat

"Running scripts is disabled on this system"
  The .bat already works around this. If you are running the .ps1 directly,
  use the .bat instead.

"Python is not installed"
  Microsoft Store, search Python 3.12, click Get, then run the .bat again.
  Both stages need it.

The 2.1 GB download stops part way
  Run it again and it carries on from where it stopped. Geofabrik rebuilds
  the file every night, so if a new version has appeared in the meantime it
  starts that one from the beginning instead of mixing the two. The filtered
  output is never half written.

"not enough disk space"
  Allow about 11 GB free. The 2.1 GB extract and the 0.8 GB location list
  are each deleted as soon as they have been used, so the peak is the 9.5 GB
  of photographs plus a margin. If the drive is tighter than that, the photo
  stage stops early and leaves 1 GB free rather than filling it.

"Another copy of the fetcher is already running"
  Only one may work on the folder at a time. Let the other finish, or close
  its window, then run this again. If no other window is open, it clears by
  itself: the lock is released the moment the other copy stops.

Photographs do not show
  They live in the photos folder next to WildFind.html. If a file is missing
  the card quietly falls back to the sketch rather than showing a broken
  image, so a half finished download still looks fine.


FOLDER
------
WildFind.html         the app
data\locations.js     the sites
data\places.js        towns for the Near box
data\photos.js        which photographs belong to which site, and their credits
data\map.js           the coastline for the Saved map (Natural Earth, public domain)
photos\               the photographs, filed in numbered subfolders
tools\FETCH DATA.bat  run this to fill it with real data
tools\fetch-data.ps1  finds Python and hands over to fetch.py
tools\fetch.py        runs both stages: downloads, filtering, the data files
tools\extract-osm.py  pulls the sites out of the OpenStreetMap extract
tools\photos.py       finds and downloads the photographs, politely
tools\state\          progress files, so a stopped fetch can resume
ATTRIBUTION.txt       picture credits, written by the fetcher
README.md             the project's front page on GitHub
tests\                checks for the fetcher scripts; not needed to use
                      the app (python tests\run_all.py)
NOTES.md              notes on how it all works, for whoever changes it next
